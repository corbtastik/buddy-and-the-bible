> **Matthew 18:6-9** *"But whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Me to sin, it would be better for him if a millstone were hung around his neck, and he were drowned in the depth of the sea. "Woe to the world because of offenses! For offenses must come, but woe to that man by whom the offense comes! If your hand or foot causes you to sin, cut it off and cast it from you. It is better for you to enter into life lame or maimed, rather than having two hands or two feet, to be cast into the everlasting fire. And if your eye causes you to sin, pluck it out and cast it from you. It is better for you to enter into life with one eye, rather than having two eyes, to be cast into hell fire."*

I believe that it is extremely interesting that Jesus mentions mutilation three times in His "gospel of the kingdom". Once in His mountain sermon (Matt. 5:29ff) then regarding divorce and remarriage (Matt. 19:12) and then here in His teaching on stumbling blocks. Only once was He speaking literally. Twice He is speaking figuratively, using the surgeon's knife to teach that there are times in life that Christians must make very hard decisions. Decisions that are difficult, inconvenient, personally undesirable and sometimes even painful -- to keep ourselves on the "straight and narrow" path to eternal life. Not just for ourselves but for those with whom we have direct contact and influence over. Don't kid yourselves: not only will we each give an account of our actions, but also how our decisions affected and influenced those around us.
