> **1 Samuel 25:21-22** Now David had said, "Surely in vain I have protected all that this fellow has in the wilderness, so that nothing was missed of all that belongs to him. And he has repaid me evil for good. May God do so, and more also, to the enemies of David, if I leave one male of all who belong to him by morning light."

David's reaction to Nabal denying his men supplies then insulting him was quite severe. He was going to eradicate Nabal, his family and his male servants. And could have done that will 400 mighty warriors. But along came Abagail.

> **1 Samuel 25:30-31** "And it shall come to pass, when the LORD has done for my lord according to all the good that He has spoken concerning you, and has appointed you ruler over Israel, that this will be no grief to you, nor offense of heart to my lord, either that you have shed blood without cause, or that my lord has avenged himself.

This woman of understanding accepted the blame of Nabal's arrogance and pled with David not to do something that would put a black mark on his reign after God made him king. Do "shed blood" in anger and avenge yourself. The lesson is that David listened to Abagail's reasoning, recognized his mistake and turn around and returned to his camp.
