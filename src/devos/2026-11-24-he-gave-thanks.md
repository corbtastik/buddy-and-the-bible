\* This is an excerpt from Charles Payne's daily devotion written this past Sunday.

> **Luke 22:19** And He took bread, gave thanks and broke it, and gave it to them, saying, "This is My body which is given for you; do this in remembrance of Me."

> This Thursday, we will gather around tables and give thanks for the blessings we have received. Today, as we gather around the Lord's table, consider the thanksgiving Jesus offered on the night before His death. He took the bread---and gave thanks. We can read those words so quickly that we scarcely pause to consider them. Jesus knew what lay ahead. He knew about the betrayal, the arrest, the scourging, and the cross. Within hours, He would pray in anguish in Gethsemane. Yet here, with the bread in His hands, He gave thanks.
>
> Imagine hearing that prayer. The One who would suffer was offering thanks. The One whose body would be given was breaking the bread and passing it to His disciples. Luke records that Jesus gave thanks without preserving the words of His prayer. We can only wonder at everything that filled His heart. But we know what He said next. "This is my body which is given for you." He understood the cost, and He willingly gave Himself for them---and for us.
>
> Sometimes I should let my troubles determine how much gratitude I express. I should think about what hurts, what worries me, and what I wish would change. Then I could picture Jesus at that table. His sorrow was real. So was His thanksgiving. His example teaches me that I can bring both before the Father.

> **Philippians 4:6-7** Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.
