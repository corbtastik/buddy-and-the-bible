> **Matthew 5:13ff** You are the salt of the earth; but if the salt loses its flavor, how shall it be seasoned? It is then good for nothing but to be thrown out and trampled underfoot by men. "You are the light of the world. A city that is set on a hill cannot be hidden. "Nor do they light a lamp and put it under a basket, but on a lampstand, and it gives light to all who are in the house. "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven

Too often when we think of our responsibility to shine light or be salt, we think in regards to big things like: Always attending worship services; teaching the gospel to others. You know things that have a lot of visibility. We want our light and salt to be clearly seen by many.

Joy once told me a story of her grandmother returning a quarter a clerk had given her as change that was more than she was due. Her grandmother told the clerk, "I'm not going to go to miss heaven over a quarter."

That attitude illustrates a very important part of our Christian influence -- the small things we do sometimes speak louder than the large things. Sometime just a smile, kind word or an expression of thanks influences someone more than seeing us attending worship services regularly. Sometimes quiet gifts of generosity speak louder than loud claims of Christianity. Sometimes kindness in times of hardship and trial are more influential than public acts of saintliness. Yes, the big things are important but we need to remember more often than not people see the little things clearer than the big things.
