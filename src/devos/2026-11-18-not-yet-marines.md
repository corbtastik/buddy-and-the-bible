In James Bradley's book "Flag Of Our Fathers" he talks about the training of the six men who raised the second flag on Iwo Jima. One interesting thing about Marine training is when the men come to camp after they have enlisted, taken their oath and left friends and family behind, they are not referred to as Marines. They are made to understand that they are not yet marines but only recruits and would not be called Marines until they had proven themselves worthy of that title.

When the Apostle Paul took pen to hand to write to the church at Colosse he begins by telling his brethren that he prays for them continually and tells them for what he prays on their behalf.

> **Colossians 1:9-12** For this reason we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you, and to ask that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding; that you may walk worthy of the Lord, fully pleasing Him, being fruitful in every good work and increasing in the knowledge of God; strengthened with all might, according to His glorious power, for all patience and longsuffering with joy; giving thanks to the Father who has qualified us to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in the light.

Paul tells them he prays that they might be filled with knowledge and spiritual understanding. To the end that they might walk worthy of the Lord. The end of knowledge must be conduct and what Paul is saying he prays that they might be filled in knowledge to the end that their character may grow. To walk worthy of the Lord is to walk in a manner comparable to Him. We cannot call ourselves Christians if we are not conducting ourselves like Christ. You can't be a Marine until you can act like one in the same way we cannot call ourselves Christ's until we are living a life that is in harmony with His will, His commands, His person.

> **1 Peter 2:21** For to this you were called, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, that you should follow His steps.
