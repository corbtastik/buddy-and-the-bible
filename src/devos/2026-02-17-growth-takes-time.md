---
> **Hebrews 5:12-14** For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need *someone* to teach you again the first principles of the oracles of God; and you have come to need milk and not solid food. For everyone who partakes *only* of milk *is* unskilled in the word of righteousness, for he is a babe. But solid food belongs to those who are of full age, *that is,* those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil. This I say, therefore, and testify in the Lord, that you should no longer walk as the rest of the Gentiles walk, in the futility of their mind, having their understanding darkened, being alienated from the life of God, because of the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their heart;

The prophet Hosea warns us that destruction is the product of ignorance (Hosea 4:6). Paul also warns that ignorance leads to rebellion against God's will and his commands (Romans 10:1-3). Ignorance is likened to darkness, and it is this darkness that we are called out of darkness into the light of knowledge and salvation (1 Peter 2:9).

But we need to remember that the acquisition of knowledge takes time. Our children are unable to learn everything overnight (even as much as we would like them to or think they can). Neither can Christians grow from spiritual babes to the perfect or mature man overnight. It takes time to acquire knowledge and time to exercise that knowledge to the point that a person can "discern" right from wrong. On the other hand, time can be the enemy of our spiritual growth. We can impede our spiritual growth in the same way we do our physical growth. If we cease "imbibing" the nourishment of God's word (through Bible study) and if we fail to "exercise ourselves unto godliness" (by practicing what we learn) time means nothing to us. We stop growing and eventually die.
