> **Acts 22:19-22** "So I said, 'Lord, they know that in every synagogue I imprisoned and beat those who believe on You. 'And when the blood of Your martyr Stephen was shed, I also was standing by consenting to his death, and guarding the clothes of those who were killing him.' Then He said to me, 'Depart, for I will send you far from here to the Gentiles.'" And they listened to him until this word, and then they raised their voices and said, "Away with such a fellow from the earth, for he is not fit to live!"

Paul's defense to the Jews is an account of his conversion. He gives account of his life persecuting the Way and how that during his endeavors he had been sent to Damascus with letters to arrest and bring back to Jerusalem all those who professed belief in Jesus. But while he was on his way Jesus appeared to him showing him that indeed he was fighting against God as he fought against Christians. He told of being led to Damascus and Ananias being sent with instructions from God for Paul. Part of those instructions included God's charge to Paul to go to the gentiles with the gospel. The audience listens very intently up to a point. But when Paul comes to a part in his defense that they do not want to hear -- they close their ears and hear no more.

Have you ever looked up the definition of preaching? One modern definition is "the art of talking in someone else's sleep!" Webster includes in it's definitions -- Boring harangue, or giving advice in an obtrusive or tedious way

We make up a lot of excuses NOT to listen to God's word but the truth of the matter is man has been refusing to listen to God since the beginning of time and it's because of that refusal sin is so prevalent in the world AND makes such great inroads into the church! People sleep in the pews so to speak, closing their minds to the proclamation and instruction of God' word. Isaiah described this attitude when centuries ago he condemned the Jews for their refusal to listen to and heed God's word.

> **Matthew 13:14-15** "And in them the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled, which says: 'Hearing you will hear and shall not understand and seeing you will see and not perceive; for the hearts of this people have grown dull. Their ears are hard of hearing, and their eyes they have closed, lest they should see with their eyes and hear with their ears, est they should understand with their hearts and turn, so that I should heal them.'

So the question the old radio preacher used to ask is pertinent to each of us "Are you listening?"
