When Peter takes pen in hand to write his first letter, he writes to the PILGRIMS in dispersion. These Pilgrims he describes as:

- The ELECT of God
- Those who have been SANCTIFIED by the Holy Spirit.
- Begotten (or born into) a LIVING HOPE.
- Who have PURIFIED THEMSELVES through their obedience.
- Who had BEEN BORN AGAIN by the WORD OF GOD.
- Who had been BAPTIZED for the purpose of salvation.

In Chapter 4 Peter describes his readers as those who have repented or ceased from sin which simply means they have "died" to sin as Jesus died to fleshly living, the same point Paul makes in Romans. And because of their change of heart toward sin, they were to spend the rest of their time in the flesh, living for God, instead of for self.

> **Romans 6:1-2** What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?

And as we consider these descriptions, we understand that Peter is writing to encourage Christians to remain faithful even in the face the various trials of life.

> **1 Peter 1:6-7** In this you greatly rejoice, though now for a little while, if need be, you have been grieved by various trials, that the genuineness of your faith, being much more precious than gold that perishes, though it is tested by fire, may be found to praise, honor, and glory at the revelation of Jesus Christ,

And one of the trials we face in our determination to do God's will is the derision and mockery of the people in the world around us. In regard to these, they think it strange that you do not run with them in the same flood of dissipation, speaking evil of you (1 Peter 4:4). Peter's warning that the "trials" of life includes the attitude the world will have towards us. Help arm us with the proper sense of awareness and determination we will need to overcome and endure. When the world looks at us and understands our faith and observes our devotion to God's commands, they indeed think it a strange thing.
