> *"For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day according to the Scriptures, and the He was seen by Cephas, then by the twelve. After that He was seen by over five hundred brethren at once, of whom the greater part remain to the present, but some have fallen asleep. After that He was seen by James, then by all the apostles. Then last of all He was seen by me also, as by one born out of due time."* (1 Corinthians 15:3-8)

What was Paul doing in these scriptures? Was his intent to record the events after the resurrection? Was it important that Christians know just who saw Jesus when? Was Paul giving preeminence to "witnesses" of His resurrection? Was there some notoriety among believers for those who actually saw Jesus after His resurrection? Or was Paul carefully and methodically setting forth evidence to affirm the truthfulness of the resurrection of Jesus? Obviously, from the context, his intent in this part of his letter is to address false teaching concerning the resurrection of the dead (v12). He does so by presenting the list of witnesses that actually saw Jesus after his death and then His resurrection. What is the purpose of the statement of verse 6 "of whom the greater part remain to the present"? Could it be so that if any question remained in the minds of his readers to the validity of his testimony, they could by reason of investigation confirm that which he wrote? Confirm the truthfulness of the gospel message.

Paul here establishes and emphasizes the importance of truth and it's relationship to Christians and Christianity. Still a great lesson for Christians today.

> *John 8:31-32 .., "If you abide in My word, you are My disciples indeed. And you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free."*
