> **John 8:3-7** Then the scribes and Pharisees brought to Him a woman caught in adultery. And when they had set her in the midst, they said to Him, "Teacher, this woman was caught in adultery, in the very act. "Now Moses, in the law, commanded us that such should be stoned. But what do You say?" This they said, testing Him, that they might have something of which to accuse Him. But Jesus stooped down and wrote on the ground with His finger, as though He did not hear. So when they continued asking Him, He raised Himself up and said to them, "He who is without sin among you, let him throw a stone at her first."

There is a saying that I hear occasionally "there, except for the grace of God, go I." That saying depicts an attitude of compassion, because it states a fundamental feeling of sympathy based upon commonality of humankind. All of mankind suffers from common weaknesses and faults and we all face the same kinds of problems. And when we are compassionate, we look at someone else with that knowledge. I'M NO DIFFERENT THAN THAT PERSON CERTAINLY NO BETTER. Now how many challenges and confrontations could be successfully overcome if we had this kind of attitude? Rather than becoming defensive, pushy or demanding. Rather than being judgmental and aloof. If we are compassionate and sympathetic, the wrongs others do to us become MINIMAL and our interest in their WELLBEING greater than our desire to RETALIATE.

> **John 8:12** Then Jesus spoke to them again, saying, "I am the light of the world. He who follows Me shall not walk in darkness, but have the light of life."
