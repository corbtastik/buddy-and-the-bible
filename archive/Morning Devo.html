Buddy Payne
When Peter takes pen in hand towrite his first letter, he writes to thePILGRIMS in dispersion. ThesePilgrims he describes as:​· The ELECT of God​· Those who have been SANCTIFIEDby the Holy Spirit.​· Begotten (or born into) a LIVINGHOPE.​· Who have PURIFIED THEMSELVESthrough their obedience.​· Who had BEEN BORN AGAIN by theWORD OF GOD.​· Who had been BAPTIZED for thepurpose of salvation.​In Chapter 4 Peter describes hisreaders as those who have repentedor ceased from sin which simplymeans they have “died” to sin asJesus died to fleshly living, the samepoint Paul makes in Romans. Andbecause of their change of hearttoward sin, they were to spend therest of their time in the flesh, livingfor God, instead of for self.​Romans 6:1-2 What shall we saythen? Shall we continue in sin thatgrace may abound? Certainly not!How shall we who died to sin live anylonger in it?​And as we consider thesedescriptions, we understand thatPeter is writing to encourageChristians to remain faithful even inthe face the various trials of life.​1Peter 1:6-7 In this you greatlyrejoice, though now for a little while,if need be, you have been grieved byvarious trials, that the genuinenessof your faith, being much moreprecious than gold that perishes,though it is tested by fire, may befound to praise, honor, and glory atthe revelation of Jesus Christ,​And one of the trials we face in ourdetermination to do God’s will is thederision and mockery of the peoplein the world around us. In regard tothese, they think it strange that youdo not run with them in the sameflood of dissipation, speaking evil ofyou (1Peter 4:4). Peter’s warningthat the “trials” of life includes theattitude the world will have towardsus. Help arm us with the propersense of awareness anddetermination we will need toovercome and endure. When theworld looks at us and understandsour faith and observes our devotionto God’s commands, they indeedthink it a strange thing.​
